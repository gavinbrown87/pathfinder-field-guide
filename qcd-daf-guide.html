The Pathfinder Field Guide · Guide

Tax-smart giving, made simple.

Two of the most powerful giving vehicles in fundraising — and the ones nonprofits most often fumble. A thinking tool for working out which fits a donor, the rules you must get right, and how to start the conversation.

The short version

Two tax-smart giving vehicles quietly move billions to nonprofits every year: QCDs — gifts made straight from an IRA, for donors 70½ and older — and DAFs, donor-advised funds. This walks you through reasoning about which fits a given donor, the rules you have to get right, and how to open the conversation — so you decide, instead of following a script.

The two vehicles at a glance

Can it go to a DAF?No. A QCD cannot be sent to a DAF, private foundation, split-interest trust, or supporting organization.—

How to reason about donor fit

Four questions steer the conversation: the donor’s age, their giving pattern, any DAF signals, and the depth of the relationship.

If the donor is 70½ or older → QCD is in play

Age-eligibility plus steady annual cash — or cash giving that dropped after retirement — is exactly where a direct IRA gift lands well. It can support you now while keeping the amount out of the donor’s adjusted gross income, and can count toward a required minimum distribution.

Rules to get right: The transfer must go directly from the IRA custodian to the organization — if it routes through the donor, the tax treatment is lost. The 2026 limit is $111,000 per individual ($222,000 for a couple with two qualifying IRAs). And a QCD cannot be sent to a DAF — if the donor also has a fund, keep the two entirely separate.

For some donors age 70½ and older, a gift directly from an IRA can be a simple, tax-efficient way to support the mission. If that may apply to you, I can share transfer instructions, and your advisor can confirm it fits your situation.

If age is unknown → confirm it first

You can’t position a QCD without knowing the donor cleared 70½ — and roughly a third of organizations don’t capture age at all. Ask a gentle discovery question; if they’re eligible, QCD moves to the front.

When you think about your giving, do you usually give from cash flow, appreciated assets, an IRA, or a donor-advised fund?

If DAF signals are present → open the DAF pathway

Signals include a mentioned financial advisor or community foundation, a prior sponsor-routed or anonymous grant, larger irregular gifts, or prior non-cash gifts. If a fund is in the picture, a grant can usually be recommended through the sponsor — often simpler to administer than a direct gift of appreciated assets.

Get processing right: Send the legal name, tax ID (EIN), and mailing details so the sponsor can process the grant. The sponsor is the legal donor of record — soft-credit the recommending donor where their identity is known. And clarify recognition preferences — don’t assume anonymity, or how they want to be thanked.

Many supporters give through donor-advised funds. If you already have one, I can send the details your sponsor needs to recommend a grant.

If both apply → keep them distinct

A donor who is 70½+ and shows DAF signals could give via a QCD and recommend a DAF grant — but the QCD can never fund the DAF. Present them as two separate options.

If the relationship is long-tenured → bridge to planned giving

A loyal donor already giving in a tax-smart, structured way is a prime legacy prospect — they’ve shown both intent and financial-planning behavior. Plant the seed without pressure. (For a newer donor, steward first, then revisit.)

You’ve already chosen a thoughtful, tax-smart way to give. Some donors in your position also choose to include the organization in their estate plans so their impact continues. Is that something that’s ever been on your mind?

Not tax or legal advice. This guide helps development staff reason about gift vehicles — it doesn’t provide individualized tax, legal, or financial advice. Use language like “may reduce taxable income” and “may be tax-efficient,” and always refer the donor to their own advisor before they act. Never tell a donor they “will save” a specific amount. Confirm all gift details in writing with the IRA custodian or DAF sponsor. When in doubt, hire a CPA — it’s cheaper than paying fines.
