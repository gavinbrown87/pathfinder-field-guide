Why onboarding makes or breaks a board member.

Most new members are recruited with a warm conversation and then handed nothing. They sit through meetings half-lost, unsure what's expected, and slowly disengage. The ones who thrive got the opposite: a real welcome, the documents that matter, a clear picture of their role, and someone to ask.

People don't disengage because they don't care. They disengage because no one showed them how to help.

This builds a one-page welcome packet you can hand any new member — so their first 90 days set them up to actually contribute.

Step 3 · The orientation

Set up a real orientation meeting.

One unhurried sit-down to walk through the mission, the money, and how the board works saves months of quiet confusion. Don't make them piece it together from meetings.

Step 4 · The must-reads

Give them the documents that matter.

Step 5 · The expectations

Be clear about what you're counting on.

Unspoken expectations are the root of most board frustration. Say it plainly up front — attendance, giving, committee work — and you'll rarely have to chase it later.

6

Step 6 · The giving expectation

Name the giving expectation, plainly.

Funders expect 100% board giving, so every member should know it's part of the role from day one. State it clearly — the participation matters more than the amount.

Step 7 · A guide

What goes into a strong welcome

Eight things that turn a new name into a real contributor:

  1. A personal welcome, not a form letter.
  2. A real orientation before meeting one.
  3. The documents they need to govern.
  4. Expectations said out loud.
  5. The giving ask, made plainly.
  6. A buddy for the "dumb questions."
  7. A clear picture of their role.
  8. A reachable goal for the first 90 days.

Onboard them well, and they'll show up for years.
